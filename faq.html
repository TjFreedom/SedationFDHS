It depends on the level of sedation. With nitrous oxide you remain fully awake but relaxed and comfortable. With oral conscious sedation you are deeply drowsy but can still respond to your dentist's instructions. IV sedation produces a sleep-like state where most patients have no memory of the procedure. General anesthesia results in complete unconsciousness. We'll discuss what to expect for your specific sedation type before your appointment.

Recovery time depends on the sedation type. Nitrous oxide wears off completely within minutes after the mask is removed — most patients drive themselves home without any issue. Oral conscious sedation and IV sedation typically take 12–24 hours to fully clear your system. During this period you should not drive, operate heavy machinery, or make important decisions. We'll provide complete written aftercare instructions before your appointment.

Coverage varies significantly by insurance plan. Nitrous oxide is partially covered by some plans, while oral sedation, IV sedation, and general anesthesia may require pre-authorization or may be covered only in specific medical circumstances. Our team will verify your insurance benefits before your appointment and provide a complete, transparent cost estimate. We also accept CareCredit financing to help make sedation dentistry accessible for every budget.

For nitrous oxide only, most patients can drive themselves home immediately after the appointment. For oral conscious sedation, IV sedation, and general anesthesia, you must have a responsible adult (18+) drive you to and from the appointment and remain with you at home for at least 24 hours. Please arrange this before booking a deeper sedation appointment — we cannot administer sedation without a confirmed companion plan in place.

For nitrous oxide, there are usually no food restrictions — though we recommend avoiding heavy meals immediately before. For oral sedation, IV sedation, and general anesthesia, you will be asked to fast (no food or drink, including water) for a specified period before your appointment — typically 6–8 hours. You will receive detailed fasting instructions during your consultation. Following these instructions is critical for your safety.

During your consultation, share a complete list of all prescription medications, over-the-counter drugs, supplements, and herbal remedies you take. Some medications — including certain blood thinners, antidepressants, and sleep aids — can interact with sedation agents. Our team will review your medication list and advise whether any adjustments are needed before your procedure. Never stop a prescribed medication without consulting your primary physician first.

The best sedation option depends on your level of anxiety, your medical history, the type and length of procedure, and your personal comfort goals. During your consultation, we'll discuss all of these factors and recommend the most appropriate option. Many patients who have never tried sedation start with nitrous oxide and discover it's all they need, while others with more significant anxiety benefit from oral or IV sedation. There's no wrong answer — just the right answer for you.
