About Our Program

What Is Sedation Dentistry — and Is It Right for You?

Sedation dentistry uses safe, well-tested medications to help patients relax during dental procedures. It's not just for the deeply anxious — it's for anyone who wants a more comfortable, efficient dental experience.

At FDHS, we offer a full spectrum of sedation levels so we can match the right approach to your medical history, the complexity of your procedure, and your personal comfort goals. From a gentle whiff of nitrous oxide for a nervous child to IV sedation for an adult undergoing complex restorations, we have you covered.

Our team reviews your complete medical history before recommending any sedation option, and a licensed clinician monitors your vital signs throughout every procedure. Safety and comfort are never compromised.

Dental Anxiety Is More Common Than You Think

An estimated 36% of Americans experience dental anxiety, and about 12% avoid the dentist entirely due to fear. Untreated dental problems lead to more serious — and more expensive — health issues down the road.

Sedation dentistry removes the barrier. You get the care you need without the fear you dread.

Our Services

Four Levels of Dental Sedation in Bear, DE

We offer every level of sedation so your treatment plan is always matched to your needs — no matter how mild or significant your anxiety may be.

😮‍💨
Level 1 — Mild

Nitrous Oxide (Laughing Gas)

Gentle relaxation that wears off in minutes

Nitrous oxide is a colorless, odorless gas administered through a small mask placed over your nose. Within a few minutes you'll feel pleasantly relaxed, warm, and sometimes mildly euphoric — while remaining fully awake and able to respond to your dentist.

  • Works in 2–3 minutes
  • Fully wears off within minutes of removal
  • Most patients can drive themselves home
  • Safe for children ages 3 and older
  • Ideal for routine cleanings, fillings, and extractions
  • No needles required
Best for: Mild dental anxiety, sensitive patients, routine procedures, children
Most Popular
💊
Level 2 — Moderate

Oral Conscious Sedation

A single pill for deep, worry-free relaxation

Oral conscious sedation involves taking a prescribed sedative medication approximately one hour before your appointment. By the time you're in the chair, you'll be in a deeply relaxed, drowsy state. You can still respond to instructions but will feel detached from the procedure — and most patients remember little or nothing afterward.

  • No IV or injections
  • Deep relaxation with minimal memory of the procedure
  • Allows multiple treatments in a single visit
  • Reduces gag reflex significantly
  • Excellent for moderate-to-severe anxiety
  • Pre-procedure health screening included
Best for: Moderate–severe anxiety, long appointments, strong gag reflex, dental phobia
Note: You will need a trusted adult to drive you home and stay with you for 24 hours.
💉
Level 3 — Deep

IV Sedation ("Sleep Dentistry")

Maximum relaxation for complex procedures

Intravenous (IV) sedation delivers medication directly into your bloodstream, allowing for precise and rapid control of sedation depth. You'll enter a sleep-like state almost immediately and have virtually no memory of the procedure. Our team can adjust the level of sedation in real time throughout your appointment.

  • Takes effect within seconds
  • Deepest level of conscious sedation
  • Dose adjusted continuously throughout treatment
  • Vital signs monitored by trained clinical staff
  • Ideal for complex or lengthy procedures
  • High amnesic effect — little to no procedure memory
Best for: Severe anxiety, multiple extractions, implants, complex restorations
Note: A responsible adult must drive you home and accompany you for 24 hours post-procedure.
🏥
Level 4 — Full

General Anesthesia

Complete unconscious sedation for specialized needs

General anesthesia renders patients fully unconscious and is reserved for those who cannot tolerate dental treatment in any other way — including patients with severe special needs, certain medical conditions, or extreme dental phobia that does not respond to lighter sedation levels.

  • Complete unconsciousness throughout procedure
  • No awareness or memory of treatment
  • Suitable for patients with special needs or disabilities
  • Allows extensive dental work in a single session
  • Administered and monitored by qualified clinicians
  • Comprehensive pre-operative evaluation required
Best for: Special needs patients, extreme phobia, very young children requiring extensive work

Who Is Sedation Dentistry For?

Sedation dentistry isn't just for people with severe fear. Many everyday dental situations benefit from a relaxed, comfortable approach.

😰

Dental Anxiety & Phobia

If fear keeps you from scheduling or completing dental appointments, sedation removes that barrier so you can get the care your oral health demands — without the dread.

🤢

Sensitive Gag Reflex

A strong gag reflex makes many procedures uncomfortable and difficult. Sedation significantly reduces this response, making even routine X-rays and impressions tolerable.

💉

Low Pain Tolerance

Some patients require more than local anesthesia to feel comfortable. Sedation complements numbing agents to ensure you feel absolutely nothing during treatment.

📋

Multiple Procedures at Once

Need several fillings, extractions, or a full mouth restoration? Sedation allows us to complete multiple procedures in a single visit, saving you time and multiple recovery periods.

🧠

Special Needs Patients

Patients with autism spectrum disorder, ADHD, Alzheimer's, cerebral palsy, or other conditions may find dental chairs and procedures overwhelming. Sedation makes treatment accessible and safe.

😔

Traumatic Past Experiences

A painful or frightening dental experience in the past can create lasting anxiety. Sedation helps you reclaim your comfort and rebuild a positive relationship with dental care.

💊

Anesthesia Resistance

Some patients have difficulty achieving full numbness with local anesthetics alone. Sedation ensures you're deeply relaxed so pain is never an issue during your procedure.

🧒

Nervous Children

Children who are too young, too anxious, or too uncooperative for routine dental work can safely receive nitrous oxide or, in some cases, stronger sedation to complete necessary treatment.

What to Expect

Your Sedation Dentistry Journey — Step by Step

We walk every patient through a clear, comfortable process from consultation to recovery. No surprises, no fear of the unknown.

01

Initial Consultation

We review your complete medical and dental history, current medications, and anxiety level. Together we identify the sedation option best suited to your needs and the procedure you're having done.

02

Pre-Procedure Preparation

You'll receive detailed written instructions tailored to your sedation type — what to eat or avoid, how to prepare transportation, and what to expect on the day of your appointment. For oral and IV sedation, we may prescribe a pre-appointment medication to help you sleep well the night before.

03

Day of Your Appointment

Arrive with a trusted companion if required. Our team will greet you warmly, administer your selected sedation, and wait until you reach optimal comfort before beginning any treatment. Your vital signs will be monitored continuously throughout the procedure.

04

Treatment

With sedation in effect, our dentists work efficiently and comfortably — often completing work that would otherwise require multiple visits. You may feel relaxed, drowsy, or fully unaware depending on your sedation level, but you are safe and monitored throughout.

05

Recovery & Aftercare

For nitrous oxide, recovery takes just minutes. For oral and IV sedation, you'll rest comfortably at our office until you're ready to leave with your designated driver. We provide written aftercare instructions and our team is available by phone if you have any questions during your recovery at home.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Sedation in Delaware

Have questions before you book? Here are answers to the questions we hear most often from patients in Bear, Glasgow, and Newark, DE.

Yes. FDHS provides safe dental sedation for patients as young as 3 years old. Nitrous oxide is the most common and safest choice for children. Our team performs a thorough medical history review, monitors vital signs continuously, and follows strict pediatric safety protocols. We'll discuss the best option for your child based on their age, health, and the procedure needed.

It depends on the level of sedation. With nitrous oxide you remain fully awake but relaxed and comfortable. With oral conscious sedation you are deeply drowsy but can still respond to your dentist's instructions. IV sedation produces a sleep-like state where most patients have no memory of the procedure. General anesthesia results in complete unconsciousness. We'll discuss what to expect for your specific sedation type before your appointment.

Recovery time depends on the sedation type. Nitrous oxide wears off completely within minutes after the mask is removed — most patients drive themselves home without any issue. Oral conscious sedation and IV sedation typically take 12–24 hours to fully clear your system. During this period you should not drive, operate heavy machinery, or make important decisions. We'll provide complete written aftercare instructions before your appointment.

Coverage varies significantly by insurance plan. Nitrous oxide is partially covered by some plans, while oral sedation, IV sedation, and general anesthesia may require pre-authorization or may be covered only in specific medical circumstances. Our team will verify your insurance benefits before your appointment and provide a complete, transparent cost estimate. We also accept CareCredit financing to help make sedation dentistry accessible for every budget.

For nitrous oxide only, most patients can drive themselves home immediately after the appointment. For oral conscious sedation, IV sedation, and general anesthesia, you must have a responsible adult (18+) drive you to and from the appointment and remain with you at home for at least 24 hours. Please arrange this before booking a deeper sedation appointment — we cannot administer sedation without a confirmed companion plan in place.

For nitrous oxide, there are usually no food restrictions — though we recommend avoiding heavy meals immediately before. For oral sedation, IV sedation, and general anesthesia, you will be asked to fast (no food or drink, including water) for a specified period before your appointment — typically 6–8 hours. You will receive detailed fasting instructions during your consultation. Following these instructions is critical for your safety.

During your consultation, share a complete list of all prescription medications, over-the-counter drugs, supplements, and herbal remedies you take. Some medications — including certain blood thinners, antidepressants, and sleep aids — can interact with sedation agents. Our team will review your medication list and advise whether any adjustments are needed before your procedure. Never stop a prescribed medication without consulting your primary physician first.

The best sedation option depends on your level of anxiety, your medical history, the type and length of procedure, and your personal comfort goals. During your consultation, we'll discuss all of these factors and recommend the most appropriate option. Many patients who have never tried sedation start with nitrous oxide and discover it's all they need, while others with more significant anxiety benefit from oral or IV sedation. There's no wrong answer — just the right answer for you.
